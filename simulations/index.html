Simulations

Interacting particle systems are easiest to get a feeling for by watching them. This page runs the driven clock and driven XY models of C. Maes and S. Shlosman, Rotating states in driven clock- and XY-models, J. Stat. Phys. 144 (2011) live in your browser. Every spin is a clock hand that interacts with its neighbours like an XY spin and is pushed to rotate by a constant drift. In two dimensions the collective rotation is destroyed by spiral defects; in three dimensions the whole system rotates coherently. The pictures follow the conventions of Y. Avni, M. Fruchart, D. Martin, D. Seara and V. Vitelli, Dynamical phase transitions in the nonreciprocal Ising model, Phys. Rev. E 111 (2025), whose nonreciprocal Ising model is closely related to the four-state clock.

The model

Each site of the periodic lattice carries a clock spin k ∈ {0,…,q−1} with angle φ = 2πk/q. It advances or retreats by one step at the rates of Maes & Shlosman (J. Stat. Phys. 144, 2011, Eq. 4), simulated exactly in continuous time:

c±(x) = p± · exp{ (β/2) Σ_y~x [ cos(φx − φy ± 2π/q) − cos(φx − φy) ] } d = log(p+/p−), p+ + p− = 1

The XY option integrates Eq. 7: dφ = d·dt − Σ sin(φx − φy) dt + (2/β)½ dW.

Reading the views

Pictures use θ = 2π(k + ½)/q, a global half-step rotation. For q = 4 the four states then sit on the diagonals, so σA = sgn cos θ and σB = sgn sin θ are two Ising spins whose cycle (++) → (−+) → (−−) → (+−) is the swap order of the nonreciprocal Ising model of Avni et al.; then J̃ = dim·β and K̃ = d/2.

A vortex is a plaquette whose four corners wind once around the clock. In 3D, voxels are drawn semi-transparent with grey encoding depth (dark = front).
